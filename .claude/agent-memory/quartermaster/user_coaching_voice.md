The developer is Will's dad and his coach as well as the app's only
maintainer. When he sends drill text it is a coaching brief, not a spec to
transcribe: he writes for adults, and it has to be rewritten into second
person, present tense, two to four short sentences a nine-year-old reads
himself.

**Why:** the whole app is built so Will uses it without help. A drill he
cannot read alone in a garden is a drill that does not happen.

**How to apply:** when he sends a list of moves, expect one or two of them to
already exist in the library under another name, and expect at least one
naming collision with an existing description. Say so plainly and name the
duplicate rather than adding it - that is the review he is asking for. Judge
each item on whether it earns a slot, not on whether it was on the list.

Progression context: as of 2026-09 he rates Will a confident dribbler who has
outgrown single-move ball mastery.
